When visiting a zoo or watching nature documentaries, it is easy to use "monkey" and "ape" interchangeably.{{< sidenote label="1" >}}Both monkeys and apes belong to the order Primates, which includes hundreds of living species worldwide.{{< /sidenote >}} In fact, they describe two distinct groups with clear physical and evolutionary differences.

![Gorilla portrait](/images/gorilla.webp)

Great apes tend to be larger, more social, and better studied than most monkey species.
{{< /marginnote >}}

## The Tail Tells the Tale

The quickest visual clue is the tail. Monkeys have tails, sometimes long and flexible, sometimes short and stubby, but they have them. Apes do not.

## Size and Intelligence

Apes are usually larger and tend to display more complex tool use and problem solving. Gorillas, chimpanzees, bonobos, and orangutans all show impressive learning abilities.

## Evolutionary Relationships

Humans are more closely related to apes than to monkeys. That shared ancestry is reflected in posture, shoulder mobility, and patterns of social behavior.

| Trait | Monkeys | Apes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tail | Usually yes | No |
| Body size | Often smaller | Often larger |
| Shoulder mobility | Limited | More flexible |

Understanding this distinction helps us appreciate the diversity of primates and the branches of our own evolutionary tree.
